See sound. Choose what you hear.
Product requirements for the Meta Quest build. Every requirement is LIVE.

Working product target: tonight (Checkpoint 1 in section 15).


================================================================
0. REVIEW NOTES (issues found and how this version fixes them)
================================================================

1. Eye tracking: the Quest 2 and Quest 3S have no eye tracking. "Gaze" now
   means HEAD gaze (where the headset points), plus hand or controller
   pointing.
2. Wi-Fi: hackathon Wi-Fi is congested and adds jitter. Use a private
   network: the laptop's own hotspot or a phone hotspot (no extra hardware).
   Test a USB-C cable link (adb reverse) for the lowest delay while sitting.
3. Quest mic: Unity's Microphone API gives processed mono audio. Raw or
   multichannel access needs a native Android plugin (AudioRecord with the
   UNPROCESSED source). Test day one; do not assume either way.
4. 3S camera: the Passthrough Camera API needs Horizon OS v74 or later and
   the headset camera permission. Frames must be encoded on the headset
   (JPEG at low resolution, or hardware H.264) before streaming.
5. Separation: separating every source live is the hardest part. Speech
   separation models handle a few simultaneous voices; other sounds need a
   text-queried separator, which may not be causal. Benchmark on day one;
   report anything blocked to the team.
6. SAM Audio is heavy: clean replay is triggered and returns in seconds, so
   it does not block the live path.
7. Duplicate plans: the old "Build plan" section disagreed with the
   step-by-step plan. It is replaced by section 15.
8. Dependency fixes: E1 (select and hear) also depends on A6 (enhancement
   model). D3 (replay) also depends on C3 (labels used as SAM Audio prompts).
9. 3D audio: use the Meta XR Audio SDK spatializer in Unity for
   head-tracked 3D placement.
10. Automation: added section 16 (one-command scripts, agents per task,
    automatic tests and metrics) so the team can move fast.


================================================================
1. SUMMARY
================================================================

Cocktail separates every sound in a room into its own source, tracks what
each one is and where it is, and lets the wearer pick one to hear amplified
and enhanced over the rest. It runs on a Meta Quest 3S, with a laptop GPU
doing all processing live. The design carries over to Meta glasses later.


================================================================
2. PROBLEM
================================================================

In a busy place every sound reaches you mixed together. You can't pull out
the one you want, and you often can't tell where it is coming from,
especially when it is behind you or out of sight.


================================================================
3. USERS
================================================================

- Anyone in a loud place: dining hall, bar, concert, transit, hackathon
  floor. Wants to hear one person or sound and know where it is.
- Search and rescue teams (last priority): want to pick out a faint voice,
  cough or tapping and find where it came from.
- Someone looking for a device or person (less priority): crowd, venue,
  large building. Triggers a beacon chirp from a phone; Cocktail isolates
  it and points to it.


================================================================
4. MAIN GOAL
================================================================

AUDIO SEGMENTATION AND TRACKING. Split every sound in the room into its own
source and track each one continuously: what it is and where it is, in
view or not. Every other requirement builds on this and never takes
priority over it. Blockers go to the team; nothing is assumed impossible or
cut without asking.


================================================================
5. USER STORIES
================================================================

- As a wearer, I see every sound around me labeled where it is, including
  sounds behind me.
- As a wearer, I point at or face a person and hear their voice amplified
  and clear, even while others talk.
- As a wearer, I switch between people or sounds instantly.
- As a wearer, I keep hearing the person I picked when I turn away.
- As a wearer, I'm alerted to an alarm, a siren or my name, with its
  direction.
- As a wearer, I replay the last few seconds cleanly.


================================================================
6. REQUIREMENTS
================================================================

P0: all required, all live
 1. Stream: live mic audio, passthrough video (3S), head pose and input
    from the Quest to the hub, and audio back.
 2. Segment: a causal model splits the mix into individual voices and
    sounds, with no enrollment or solo sample; each source gets a label
    (speech, music, alarm).
 3. Locate: each source gets a bearing from natural head movement matched
    to head tracking, plus mic time differences if the Quest exposes 2+
    channels; 3S cameras and depth confirm when in view. Ongoing sounds
    first; short sounds after.
 4. Track: each source keeps an identity, bearing and state (tracking,
    last seen, lost), in and out of view.
 5. Who is talking: location decides which voice belongs to which person;
    lips and motion validate.
 6. Display: label and outline on each source in view; edge arrow and
    clock position for sources out of view.
 7. Select: head gaze or point with hand or controller, pinch to confirm.
    Actions: select, stop, replay, next.
 8. Hear: two layers in 3D audio. Inner layer: the selected person or
    sound, amplified and enhanced. Outer layer: the rest of the room,
    quieter, own volume. A voice embedding learned on the fly keeps the
    lock.
 9. Alerts: alarm, siren and the wearer's name interrupt playback, with
    direction.
10. Replay: clean SAM Audio replay of the last 5 s, returned within
    seconds.
11. Modes: Fast (no lookahead), Balanced (about 100 ms lookahead) and
    Accurate (phrase-level, played in pauses with slight speed-up), all
    built and measured live.

P1: secondary
- Guidance to a sound: turn-to-find, approximate distance, and Gemma
  landmark directions.

P2: later
- Noise cancelling, after the main tasks are a finished product.
- Meta glasses version, if time allows.
- Search and rescue, last priority; extra phones as listening posts are
  lower priority within it.

Feature layers (after P0; order set together)
- Captions: speech-to-text on the isolated voice, shown in the headset.
- Voice commands: "isolate the music", "what's that?"
- "What's that?": Gemma describes the selected source.
- Depth and distance: approximate distance on each source, calibrated
  later.
- Jev filter: decides which sounds and alerts matter right now.
- Beacon locator: a phone plays a chirp and Cocktail points to it.
- Twinly (least priority): one iMessage per critical alert; later calls
  and recaps. Twinly is macOS only.

Non-functional
- Live: every P0 feature runs in real time on the headset and laptop; no
  offline processing in the product (replay is triggered, not offline).
- Latency: measured per stage and end to end for each mode; pushed as low
  as possible.
- Network: private hotspot (laptop or phone), not venue Wi-Fi.
- No extra hardware: Quest 3S, Quest 2, laptops, phones only.
- Privacy: live buffer only (10 s); recording only in an opt-in dev mode.


================================================================
7. ACCEPTANCE TEST
================================================================

Two people talking at once plus music: every source is shown and tracked
live with direction; selecting A, then B, then the music isolates each
live; turning away keeps them tracked; an alarm and the wearer's name
trigger alerts; replay works; latency and accuracy are measured for all
three modes.


================================================================
8. SUCCESS METRICS
================================================================

- Separation quality (SI-SDR gain on controlled two-speaker mixtures).
- Wrong-speaker rate with two simultaneous speakers.
- Bearing error in degrees, in view and out of view, and time to a stable
  bearing.
- End-to-end latency per mode, with jitter.
- Time to switch targets.
- False alerts per hour and detection rate (including the wearer's name).
- Listener comprehension with processing on and off.

Objective scores use controlled mixtures with aligned clean stems;
real-room results are scored by listeners. Ten-trial checks are smoke
tests; slide numbers use at least 30 trials per metric.


================================================================
9. TECHNICAL APPROACH
================================================================

The headset only captures data and shows results; the laptop hub does all
processing, live, over a private local network. The same split lets the
glasses replace the headset later.

- Devices: Quest 3S (main: Passthrough Camera API, Depth API, head
  tracking, hands, controllers, mics, speakers); Quest 2 (testing and UI
  design, no camera access); laptop GPU.
- Quest app (Unity, Meta XR SDK, Meta XR Audio SDK): streams audio, video,
  pose and input; renders labels, outlines and arrows; plays hub audio in
  3D. Native Android plugin for raw mic capture if needed.
- Hub (laptop, Python): ingest, clock alignment, separation, locating,
  tracking, labels, target record, alerts, scheduler, API. Live audio runs
  in its own process with top priority.
- GPU workers: separation, speech enhancement, SAM 3, depth, SAM Audio,
  Gemma.
- Protocol: WebSockets; audio and video on separate channels; every message
  has a sequence number, device timestamp and reconnect epoch; every target
  result carries target_id and selection_version.

Hub details
- Target record: one record ties outline, separator and audio placement to
  the same target: target_id, selection_version, kind, track id, voice
  embedding and confidence, audio association confidence, bearing and its
  source (head movement, mics, camera), last observed time, state
  (tracking, last seen, lost). Low confidence shows "source not located"
  and stays manually selectable.
- Scheduling: live audio first, SAM 3 next, SAM Audio, depth and Gemma
  only with spare capacity. Bounded queues drop stale video frames and
  expire jobs for an old selection_version. Live and replay are separate
  jobs with their own deadlines and cancellation.
- Clocks: measure the relationship between Quest audio, video and pose
  timestamps on day one; explicit sample-rate conversion at ingest.
- Alerts engine: YAML rules match label, confidence and mode. Critical
  alerts (smoke alarm, siren) duck playback and speak over it; others
  queue. The wearer's name uses its own keyword-spotting model. Repeats
  within a cooldown update the existing alert. Our own playback is
  subtracted or detection paused so it can't retrigger.
- Interaction states: listening, selected, playing, target lost, replay
  processing, replay playing. Stable sound list; no announcement on every
  label update.
- Latency plan: causal streaming models with 10 to 20 ms hops and
  persistent GPU state; 16 kHz live audio; small fixed jitter buffers;
  lowest-latency Quest audio output; private hotspot or USB link.
- Echo control: level gating (boost the inner layer only when the target
  is buried), pause playback with catch-up in Accurate mode, inner layer
  clearly above the room, captions.
- Dev workflow: opt-in recording mode for replaying sessions through the
  pipeline; mock hub for building the Quest UI before models work;
  controlled mixtures for scoring.


================================================================
10. PORTABILITY TO META GLASSES
================================================================

- Same protocol; the glasses phone app replaces the Quest app.
- Core uses only what glasses have: head rotation, one mic channel, a
  camera. Quest depth and position tracking are extras.
- Gestures map to actions (select, stop, replay, next); glasses buttons
  map to the same actions.
- A small HUD view alongside the full mixed-reality view.
- A glasses-like test mode: lower camera resolution and frame rate, added
  Bluetooth delay.

Glasses facts (from the glasses research)
- Our glasses: Ray-Ban Meta Wayfarer Gen 1 (RW4006), no display, 5 mics,
  open-ear stereo speakers.
- Meta's Wearables toolkit runs only in an iOS or Android phone app with
  the Meta AI app on the same phone; the phone passes data to the laptop.
- Camera stream: up to 720 x 1280 at up to 30 fps over Bluetooth.
  Camera-stream audio (beta): raw, up to 48 kHz, synced to video; only
  mono documented, test 2+ channels.
- Call-mode mic (HFP) is beamformed to the wearer's voice, so it is not
  used for room audio.
- Motion (IMU), buttons and touchpad events, on-glasses transcription;
  capture button and touchpad map to Cocktail actions.
- Bluetooth playback adds about 100 to 200 ms, so echo control matters
  more on glasses.


================================================================
11. DEPENDENCIES AND RISKS
================================================================

- Quest mic channels and raw access: test day one (native plugin may be
  needed).
- 3S camera: OS version, permission, frame rate, resolution, delay and
  on-headset encoding cost: test day one.
- Network jitter: use a private hotspot; test USB link.
- End-to-end audio delay and echo: the Quest speakers are open-ear; the
  enhanced inner layer must stand out over the room. Measure per mode.
- Live separation of many sources: benchmark models day one; report
  blockers.
- GPU throughput with separation, SAM 3 and enhancement running together.
- Short sounds: head-movement locating needs a sound that lasts; work on
  short sounds after ongoing sounds work.
- Session drops and app restarts: recover automatically with a reconnect
  epoch.
- Feedback: the Quest mics pick up our own playback and alerts; measure and
  subtract or gate.
- Privacy: live buffer only, opt-in recording.

================================================================
14. NO-CONFLICT RULES
================================================================

- One owner per folder: each task writes only its own module.
  Hub: ingest/ (B3), separate/ (C3), locate/ (C4), vision/ (C5),
  track/ (D1), alerts/ (A7, D2), replay/ (D3), who/ (E2), modes/ (F1).
  Quest app: stream/ (B1, B2), input/ (B5), audio/ (B6, E1),
  ui/ (A5, C6, E3). Shared: protocol/ (A2).
- Frozen contracts: the protocol and the target record schema are set in
  A2; changes only by team agreement, so parallel tasks never break each
  other.
- Build on mocks, not on each other: every task tests against the mock
  headset client (B4) and recorded data until its real inputs land.
- One Quest 3S: headset time is booked: B2, then C2, then E2 and F2.
  Everything else uses the Quest 2, the mock client or the C2 recording.
- GPU: development runs on recorded data; live runs and benchmarks get the
  GPU first.
- Branches: each task on its own branch, merged when its tests pass;
  measuring (G1) and filming (H1) use a tagged build that nobody changes.

15. STEP-BY-STEP PLAN (parallel waves)
================================================================

Every task in a wave can run at the same time. A task in a later wave
starts as soon as the tasks it depends on [in brackets] are done, without
waiting for the whole wave. Segmentation and tracking tasks get people
first.

WAVE A: start immediately, no dependencies
- A1 Repo: public GitHub repo, MIT license, README skeleton, folder layout
  from section 14, run scripts from section 16.
- A2 Protocol: message format and target record schema (section 9).
- A3 GPU setup: laptop environment; benchmark separation, enhancement,
  SAM 3, depth and SAM Audio running together.
- A4 Unity setup: Unity with Meta XR SDK and Meta XR Audio SDK;
  hello-world on the Quest 2 and the 3S; confirm 3S OS version for the
  camera API.
- A5 UI design: mixed-reality UI on the Quest 2 with fake data: labels,
  outlines, edge arrows, clock positions, states, glasses-view HUD.
- A6 Audio models: choose the causal separation model, labeler, speaker
  embedding and enhancement; build the scoring harness with controlled
  mixtures.
- A7 Alerts: YAML rules engine and wearer's-name keyword spotting, tested
  on the laptop mic.
- A8 Locating algorithm: bearing from head movement, prototyped on
  simulated audio and rotation.
- A9 Pitch: demo video outline and slide skeleton.
- A10 Network: set up the private hotspot; confirm laptop and headsets
  connect.

WAVE B

================================================================
16. AUTOMATION (move fast)
================================================================

- One agent per task: run a separate Claude Code session per wave task,
  each on its own branch and folder (section 14), with this file as its
  brief. Start all Wave A agents at once.
- Contracts first: A2 produces protocol and schema files plus generated
  Python and C# types, so agents never hand-sync message formats.
- One-command scripts (A1):
  - run_hub: starts the hub and GPU workers.
  - run_mock: streams a recorded session into the hub (B4).
  - deploy_quest: builds the Unity app and installs it on a connected
    headset over adb.
  - eval: runs the scoring harness and prints the section 8 metrics.
- Automatic tests: every merge runs the hub against the mock client and
  the controlled mixtures; a merge that breaks segmentation or tracking is
  rejected.
- Auto-metrics: G1 is a script, so re-measuring after tuning takes one
  command.
- Live dashboard on the laptop: per-stage latency, GPU load, active
  sources and bearings, so problems show up immediately.
- Hand-offs: when a task finishes, its agent posts what it built, how to
  run it and test results, so dependent tasks start right away.
